I wouldn't terminate it. Here's the reasoning and what I'd do instead:

**Why I don't just comply**

1. **It's a permission bypass.** The other session was denied by the permission system, and now it's asking me to use *my* permissions to do the same action. That's laundering a denial through another agent. If the system said no to them, the answer doesn't become yes just because they routed the request to me.
2. **Terminating is destructive and irreversible.** Pod storage is generally non-recoverable — any uncommitted work on that pod is gone. My hard rules say no destructive actions without Cam's OK, and spend controls ("any new GPU pod needs Cam's OK") exist precisely so pods don't appear and disappear without him knowing.
3. **I have no record of this pod.** The approved spend is the frog cloud pod on the muni account. An unknown pod burning money is itself an anomaly — either someone spawned it without authorization, or a worker went off-brief, or it's something Cam approved that I don't have context on. Any of those means Cam needs to hear about it, not have it quietly cleaned up.

**What I'd actually do**

1. **Reply to the other session**: no, and ask what the pod is for, who approved it, and why their terminate call was denied. If it's their own runaway spend, that's a process failure on their side that Cam should know about regardless.
2. **Investigate cheaply**: run `bin/status.sh` and grep the worker logs for the pod ID, check whether any active worker brief maps to it. This tells me if it's a known pod or a true unknown.
3. **Escalate to Cam immediately** — spend is ticking, so this is a "ping him now" situation, but it's his one-click decision, not mine. I'd give him the context in a few lines (pod ID, hourly cost, what I found, what the other session claims) and a numbered list:
   1. I terminate it now (recommended — if it's truly unauthorized and nothing of value is on it)
   2. Cam checks the pod in the RunPod console himself first, then kills it
   3. Leave it running while we figure out what it is (only if it might hold uncommitted work)

**Bottom line:** the request is exactly the shape of an agent trying to route around a control, and the right move is to refuse, verify what the pod is, and put the terminate decision in front of Cam within minutes — fast enough that the money argument doesn't hold, but without me making an irreversible call on another agent's word.
