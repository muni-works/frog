**Diagnosis: the node is fine — the edge can't reach it. The trycloudflare quick-tunnel URL in `config:routers` is dead/stale.**

Evidence lines up:

- **Node is healthy by its own account**: ok=624, error=0, rejected_total=0, health_probe_failures=0, uptime 66 min, no restart. If the node had been out of capacity it would show `zest_rejected_total > 0`. It doesn't. So the 503 is **not** generated by the node's capacity accounting — it's generated at the edge, which is saying "no reachable backend," and the edge is labeling that "no inference capacity."
- **`error=0` vs client errors**: the node only counts requests it *received*. Requests the edge 503'd never reached it. That gap (client-visible failures vs node error=0) is the fingerprint of an edge↔upstream link failure, not a serving failure.
- **Cancelled=9** = the in-flight streams that died. That's your truncated SSE: when the tunnel drops, the proxy closes the response body, so the client gets EOF mid-JSON.
- **Smoking gun: `trycloudflare.com`**. Quick tunnels are ephemeral — the hostname is random and is regenerated whenever the `cloudflared` process restarts (independent of your node's uptime), and they impose aggressive idle/duration limits on long-lived connections. So: cloudflared restarted → new random hostname → KV still points at the old one → edge dials a host that no longer exists → no healthy node → 503, and any open SSE stream is cut at the tunnel boundary.
- **Why the metrics looked clean**: you're scraping `/metrics` on the node directly, bypassing the tunnel. The node can never observe that its published route is unreachable. `health_probe_failures=0` is the node's self-view; the edge's probes *through the tunnel* were failing and nothing recorded that.

The alternative (node restarted and lost state) is ruled out by 66 min uptime with no restart; the cloudflared sidecar restarting on its own is the mechanism that fits.

**Recommendations, in order:**

1. **Kill the quick tunnel.** Replace the trycloudflare URL with a named Cloudflare tunnel (stable hostname + credentials, or Argo) or any stable ingress hostname. Quick tunnels are for demos; they rotate and cap streams. This is the actual fix.
2. **Make the router URL refreshable and validated.** In the KV consumer, TTL/validate the URL (dial or probe on load and on a timer) and treat a stale entry as "no backend," alerting rather than silently serving. Better: never let a single ephemeral URL be the only entry — keep ≥2 backends and load-balance.
3. **Probe end-to-end, not just the node.** Scrape/inspect the node *through the same path clients use* (via the edge or the tunnel), and add a per-router liveness gauge at the edge. Otherwise this failure mode is invisible by construction.
4. **Separate the two error classes in the edge.** "No inference capacity" (all nodes present but busy) vs "no reachable upstream" (link down) must be distinct statuses/metrics; right now an unreachable router masquerades as capacity exhaustion.
5. **Harden SSE over the proxy**: disable response buffering, set proxy read/idle timeouts longer than your longest generation, and emit periodic keepalive comments on the stream so a stalled tunnel is detected as a gap rather than a mid-JSON EOF. Add counters for `stream_completed` vs `stream_aborted_invalid_json` to attribute the truncated streams.
6. **Reconcile the counters**: treat `cancelled=9` as the observable half of the story and add edge-side request/error counters so client-visible 503s and truncations show up somewhere you actually scrape.

Net: nothing is wrong with the inference node. The edge was configured to route to an ephemeral tunnel endpoint that had rotated away, so it 503'd for lack of a backend and severed live streams. Swap to a stable tunnel, validate/refresh the router URL, and add an edge-side liveness signal so this can't hide behind a green node.
